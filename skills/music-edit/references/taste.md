# What reviews taught

From two finished edits: an F1 movie edit (32 drafts) and an anime edit (17 drafts). Treat these as good defaults until the editor says otherwise. Each one came from a specific rejection, so the reason is included.

## What works

- **Carry-over cuts.** The subject stays in front while the next shot's background arrives behind it, then that shot takes over. The editor asked for "more of these" in every edit. They keep the eye on the action while the picture changes.
- **Transitions that suck you in.** Push through the cut toward the action. Side swipes that fight the footage's direction get rejected.
- **Text pinned to objects,** moving with the thing it sits beside, on separate spots at sizes that follow the song. Tilted to match an object when it sits on one, such as a blade.
- **Big words behind a character,** set a little away from them, tucked in rather than buried.
- **Effects tied to something in the song or story,** such as a glint running along a blade on the beat, or an echo on the last word that follows the voice.
- **A tense build, then full action.** Shots that hold before the drop make the drop hit.
- **Hard transitions** into the song and on the drop.
- **SAM 2 cutouts.** The editor called them "perfect" compared with every earlier method.

## What gets rejected

- **Effects hiding the action.** "I'm not able to see what's going on in the fight." Keep most frames after the drop clean. Black-and-white frames, heavy glow and glitches on every cut all went.
- **Text glitching through a character.** This came up three times. Soft cutouts go see-through, layers that stop early make words pop in front, and small words get chopped. Use hard mattes, a full-shot layer and small words kept clear, and run the audit.
- **The first text on screen cut out.** Nothing has established the trick yet, so it reads as an error. Pin it beside the subject instead.
- **Rough cutouts** that keep chunks of background, and floating cut-out helmets.
- **Slowed clips and repeated frames.** 24 fps footage reads as laggy when stretched.
- **Per-beat shake and kicks,** and text moving with camera punches. Save punches for bar lines.
- **Moving black bars,** or effects drawn over them. Fix the bars on top of everything.
- **Text over faces.**
- **Shots that break story logic.** A sword held backwards, a sword appearing from nowhere at a shot's end, a line of dialogue followed by an unrelated yell. Check the ends of shots.
- **Several similar face shots in a row.**
- **Boosted isolated dialogue.** It sounds wrong, so cut the line instead. Don't dip music wider than the spoken words.
- **Words cut off at the end.** Extend the edit so the last word finishes.

## How the editor reviewed

They judged by ear and at full speed, and caught what stills and level measurements miss. They named timestamps, and were usually right about the frame. When asked "is that from the footage or from us?", check the source before answering. They liked being asked "what do you hear?" when a fix misses twice. They also wanted to know the reason behind choices: every number should come from a measurement or be called a starting value.
